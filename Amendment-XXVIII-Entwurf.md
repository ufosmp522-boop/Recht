# Entwurf: Article [XXVIII] der US-Verfassung
## „Equal Vote and Fair Districts Amendment“

> **Hinweise zum Entwurf**
> - Der Verfassungstext ist auf Englisch, weil eine US-Verfassungsänderung nur so Wirkung hätte. Erläuterungen und Missbrauchsprüfung sind auf Deutsch.
> - Werte in **[eckigen Klammern]** sind politische Stellschrauben, zum Beispiel Schwellenwerte oder Fristen. Sie lassen sich ändern, ohne dass die Struktur kippt.
> - Dies ist die **bereits gehärtete Fassung**: Die in Teil C gefundenen Schlupflöcher sind im Text schon geschlossen. Teil C sagt jeweils, wo.

---

## Teil A – Zur Frage „Verhältniswahl für das Präsidentenamt“

Ein einzelnes Amt lässt sich nicht proportional aufteilen, denn es gibt nur einen Präsidenten. Der Kern der Verhältniswahl ist das **gleiche Gewicht jeder Stimme**. Das setzt der Entwurf so um:

1. **Eine landesweite Direktwahl.** Alle Stimmen kommen in einen einzigen nationalen Topf, jede Stimme zählt gleich viel. Swing States und Winner-takes-all auf Staatenebene gibt es nicht mehr.
2. **Rangfolgewahl mit Mehrheitserfordernis** (Instant Runoff). Wer gewinnt, braucht die absolute Mehrheit. Den Spoiler-Effekt und Sieger mit 35 % gibt es dadurch nicht.

Eine *proportionale Verteilung der Wahlleute* nach dem Maine/Nebraska-Modell wäre die Alternative. Sie würde das Electoral College aber beibehalten und damit dem ersten Ziel widersprechen.

Die echte Verhältniswahl wirkt im Entwurf dort, wo sie sinnvoll ist: Section 12 erlaubt die Wahl des Repräsentantenhauses und der Staatsparlamente nach Verhältniswahlrecht in Mehrpersonenwahlkreisen.

## Teil B – Verfassungstext

### Article [XXVIII]

**Section 1. Abolition of the Electoral College.**

1. The second, third, and fourth clauses of Section 1 of Article II, the Twelfth Article of amendment, Section 4 of the Twentieth Article of amendment, and the Twenty-third Article of amendment are repealed. No electors for President or Vice President shall be appointed.
2. Wherever this Constitution refers to electors for President or Vice President, or to their choice or appointment, the reference shall be read as a reference to the election of the President and Vice President under this article.

**Section 2. Direct Popular Election.**

1. The President and Vice President shall be elected together, as candidates on a joint ticket, by the direct vote of the people of the United States. All votes shall be tabulated as a single national total and shall have equal weight, without regard to the place where they are cast.
2. The election shall be held on the same day throughout the United States, as fixed by law. Congress may by uniform law provide for voting before that day.
3. No person constitutionally ineligible to the office of President shall be eligible to that of Vice President. No person shall be a candidate on more than one ticket.

**Section 3. Right to Vote.**

1. Every citizen of the United States who is eighteen years of age or older shall have the right to vote for President and Vice President, whether residing in a State, in the District constituting the seat of Government of the United States, in any territory or possession of the United States, or abroad.
2. This right shall not be denied, except by a uniform law of Congress applying to [persons while serving a sentence of imprisonment for a felony, or to] persons whom a court has adjudged, after notice and hearing, to lack the capacity to vote. No State shall impose any other qualification for voting in this election, nor extend the right to vote in this election to any person not qualified under this section.
3. No law, regulation, or practice shall burden the exercise of this right beyond what is reasonably necessary to verify the identity and eligibility of voters and the integrity of the ballot. No person shall be removed from a list of voters except under uniform standards established by law, with notice and an opportunity to contest the removal.

**Section 4. Ranked Ballots; Majority Requirement.**

1. Each voter may rank the tickets appearing on the ballot in order of preference. A voter may rank as many or as few tickets as the voter wishes; a ballot marking a single ticket shall count as ranking that ticket first.
2. The votes shall be tabulated nationally in successive rounds. In each round, every ballot shall count as one vote for the highest-ranked ticket on it that has not been eliminated; a ballot ranking no such ticket shall not be counted in that or any later round. If a ticket receives more than one-half of the votes counted in a round, its candidates are elected. Otherwise, the ticket with the fewest votes in that round is eliminated and a new round is tabulated. Congress may by law provide for the simultaneous elimination of all tickets whose combined votes are fewer than the votes of the next-lowest ticket.
3. If two or more tickets are tied for elimination, the ticket with the fewest votes in the first round shall be eliminated; if they remain tied, the ticket to be eliminated shall be chosen by lot in public. If, after all recounts, the two final tickets have received an equal number of votes, a new election between those two tickets only shall be held on a date fixed by law.
4. Rules for counting ballots that are marked irregularly shall be uniform throughout the United States and shall give effect to every preference the voter has clearly expressed.

**Section 5. Ballot Access and Eligibility.**

1. A ticket shall appear on the ballot throughout the United States if it is nominated (a) by a political party whose ticket received at least [one] percent of the votes counted in the first round of the preceding presidential election, or (b) by a petition signed by eligible voters numbering at least [one-tenth of one percent] of the votes counted in that round, or such lower number as Congress may by law provide. Signatures shall be verified under uniform national standards. Neither the United States nor any State shall impose any other requirement for appearing on the ballot, except reasonable and uniform deadlines fixed by law.
2. Whether a candidate possesses the qualifications for office required by this Constitution shall be determined once, for the entire United States, in a proceeding provided by law, subject to expedited review by the Supreme Court. No State shall exclude a ticket from the ballot, or refuse to count votes cast for it, on the ground that a candidate is ineligible, unless such a determination has become final.
3. If a candidate dies, becomes incapacitated, or is finally determined to be ineligible before the election, a substitute may be designated in the manner provided by law, and votes cast for the ticket shall count for the ticket as reconstituted. A candidate may withdraw voluntarily, and be replaced, only before the date fixed by law for the certification of ballots.

**Section 6. Administration, Certification, and Counting.**

1. The States shall conduct the election within their borders in accordance with this article and with uniform national standards, which Congress shall establish by law, for registration, ballots, voting, counting, auditing, recounts, and certification. Until Congress so provides, State law consistent with this article shall apply. Congress shall provide for the conduct of the election in the District constituting the seat of Government, in the territories and possessions, among citizens abroad, and in any State that fails to conduct it.
2. Each State shall, by a date fixed by law, certify the results of its count, together with all data needed for the national tabulation, including the rankings recorded on the ballots. Certification is a ministerial duty, and no officer or body shall refuse or delay it except as required by a lawful recount or a court order. If a State has not certified by the date fixed, any voter or candidate may sue to compel certification. Until certification, the official returns of the lowest level of canvass shall be used for the national tabulation.
3. The national tabulation shall be conducted in public, in the manner provided by law, on the basis of the certified results. All data used shall be published in a form that permits independent verification. If the difference between the two tickets in the final round is less than [one-half of one percent] of the votes counted in that round, a national recount shall be conducted.
4. Congress shall meet in joint session on the [sixth] day of January following the election, or on such other day as it shall by law appoint, and the President of the Senate shall announce the result of the national tabulation as finally determined. This act is ministerial. Neither House, nor any member or officer of either House, shall have power to object to, reject, or alter any certified result or the result of the national tabulation.
5. Disputes concerning the election shall be decided by the courts of the United States, under expedited procedures provided by law that ensure final decision by the Supreme Court before the day of the joint session.
6. Neither the United States nor any State, nor any officer thereof, shall postpone, cancel, or suspend the election in the whole or in any part of the country. Congress may by uniform law provide only for extending the time for voting in an area where voting is prevented by natural disaster, attack, or similar emergency.

**Section 7. Stability of Election Rules.**

No law, regulation, or rule altering the conduct of the election of President and Vice President, the qualifications of voters, the requirements for ballot access, or the method of tabulation shall apply to any election held within [twelve] months after its adoption, except as necessary to comply with a final judgment of a court or with clause 6 of Section 6.

**Section 8. Prohibition of Partisan Gerrymandering.**

1. No district for the election of Representatives in Congress, of members of a State legislature, or of members of any elected body of a political subdivision of a State shall be drawn for the purpose of favoring or disfavoring any political party, candidate, or incumbent, or any group of voters because of their political views or voting behavior.
2. Districts shall be drawn in accordance with the following criteria, which take precedence in the order listed:
   (a) Districts shall have equal population, counted as the total number of persons according to the most recent decennial census, with persons confined in correctional facilities counted at their last residence before confinement, as Congress shall provide. Districts for Representatives in Congress shall be as nearly equal in population as practicable; other districts shall not deviate by more than [one] percent from the ideal population.
   (b) Districts shall comply with this Constitution and with the laws of the United States protecting the voting rights of racial and language minorities.
   (c) Districts shall satisfy the requirement of partisan fairness under Section 9.
   (d) Districts shall be contiguous.
   (e) Districts shall respect the boundaries of political subdivisions and of communities of interest, minimizing their division. A community of interest is a population sharing common social or economic interests; it shall not be defined by reference to political party, voting behavior, or relationship to any incumbent or candidate.
   (f) Districts shall be compact.
3. The residence of any incumbent or candidate shall not be considered in drawing districts. Data on party affiliation and election results shall be used only to assess compliance with subclauses (b) and (c) of clause 2.

**Section 9. Measure of Partisan Fairness.**

1. The partisan bias of a districting plan is the number of districts, beyond one-half of all districts, that a political party would win if it received exactly one-half of the statewide two-party vote. It shall be computed for every general election for President, Senator, and Governor held in the State during the preceding [ten] years. For each such election, the vote of the two parties whose candidates received the most votes statewide shall be shifted by the same number of percentage points in every district until each party holds exactly one-half of the statewide two-party vote. The partisan bias of the plan is the average of the results for these elections, taken as an absolute value.
2. A plan applying to single-member districts is presumed invalid if its partisan bias exceeds the greater of one district or [five] percent of the total number of districts. This presumption may be rebutted only by clear and convincing evidence that no plan complying with subclauses (a), (b), and (d) of clause 2 of Section 8 could have a partisan bias not exceeding that threshold.
3. A plan that satisfies the threshold is nonetheless invalid if it was drawn for a purpose prohibited by clause 1 of Section 8.
4. Congress may by law prescribe additional measures of partisan fairness, or a lower threshold. It may not raise the threshold or otherwise weaken the protections of this article.

**Section 10. Independent Redistricting Commissions.**

1. In each State, districts for Representatives in Congress and for the State legislature shall be drawn exclusively by an independent redistricting commission. Districts for the elected bodies of political subdivisions shall be drawn by the commission or by an authority designated by State law; in either case Sections 8, 9, 11, and 13 shall apply.
2. The commission shall consist of [fifteen] members. [Five] shall be affiliated with the party whose candidate for President received the most votes in the State at the preceding presidential election. [Five] shall be affiliated with the party whose candidate received the second-most votes. [Five] shall be affiliated with neither of those parties. Affiliation shall be determined by party registration, participation in party primaries, political contributions, and public political activity during the preceding [five] years. A person affiliated with either of those parties at any time during that period is eligible only for that party's group. A person affiliated with both of those parties during that period is ineligible.
3. No person shall serve on the commission who, within the [ten] years preceding appointment, has been any of the following: a candidate for or holder of a partisan elective office; an officer or paid employee of a political party, campaign, or political committee; a registered lobbyist; a member of the staff of a legislator or legislature; or a contributor to candidates, parties, or political committees of more than [an amount fixed by law, not exceeding one thousand dollars in any year, adjusted for inflation]. Nor shall any spouse, parent, child, or sibling of a current holder of an office elected from districts drawn by the commission serve on it. For [ten] years after service, no member shall be a candidate for any partisan office in the State, be employed by a legislature, party, or campaign, or act as a lobbyist.
4. Applicants' eligibility shall be determined by a nonpartisan officer designated by State law, subject to judicial review. From the eligible applicants in each group, [twenty] shall be selected by public lot. The leaders of the two largest parties in each house of the State legislature may each strike [two] of them without stating reasons. The members shall then be selected by public lot from the remaining applicants of each group. Vacancies shall be filled by lot from the remaining applicants of the same group.
5. Adoption of a plan requires the votes of a majority of all members, including at least [three] members of each group.
6. If no plan is adopted by the date fixed by law, each member may submit one plan. The court having jurisdiction under Section 13 shall determine which submitted plans comply with Sections 8 and 9 and shall adopt, from among them, the plan with the lowest partisan bias. If two or more such plans are tied, the court shall adopt the one that divides the fewest political subdivisions, and if they remain tied, one chosen by lot. If no submitted plan complies, the court shall adopt a plan drawn under the same criteria by a neutral expert it appoints. The State legislature shall have no role in drawing the plan.
7. The commission's meetings, deliberations, data, draft plans, and communications concerning districting shall be public. No member shall communicate about districting with any legislator, party, candidate, or agent of any of them, except in a public meeting or by a written submission that is published.
8. The State shall provide the commission with sufficient funds, staff, and data, not less in real terms than for the preceding redistricting. The courts shall remedy any failure to do so.
9. A plan adopted under this section takes effect without the approval of the legislature or the Governor and is not subject to veto, referendum, or initiative. No law or constitutional provision of a State, whether adopted by its legislature or by its people, shall authorize the use of any plan not adopted in accordance with this article.

**Section 11. Timing and Judicial Review.**

1. Districts shall be drawn once following each decennial census, within [six] months after the census data are made available. A plan so adopted shall remain in effect until districts are drawn following the next decennial census. It may be changed earlier only to remedy a violation found by a final judgment, and then only to the extent necessary.
2. Any person entitled to vote in an election under a plan may challenge it within [forty-five] days after its adoption. A challenge based on evidence that could not reasonably have been obtained within that period may be brought later. Challenges shall be decided expeditiously. A plan that a court finds likely to violate Section 8 or 9 shall not be used in any election; the court shall instead order the use of a plan selected under clause 6 of Section 10. The proximity of an election shall not justify the use of a plan found likely to violate this article.

**Section 12. Methods of Election.**

A State entitled to more than one Representative in Congress shall elect them either from single-member districts, or from districts electing at least [five] members each by a method of proportional representation prescribed by law; in either case Sections 8 through 11 shall apply. No State shall elect more than one Representative from the same district by a method under which a plurality or majority of voters may win all of the seats. The same rules shall apply to the election of members of each State legislature.

**Section 13. Enforcement.**

1. Claims arising under this article are justiciable. No court shall decline to decide such a claim on the ground that it presents a political question.
2. Any person entitled to vote in an election affected by a violation of this article may sue to enforce it. The courts of the United States shall have jurisdiction of such suits, and Congress shall provide for expedited review by the Supreme Court. Neither a State nor any officer thereof shall be immune from suit for declaratory or injunctive relief under this article.
3. Congress shall have power to enforce this article by appropriate legislation. No law shall diminish the rights secured by this article.
4. The rights secured by this article shall not be denied or abridged by any means, direct or indirect.

**Section 14. Effective Date and Transition.**

1. Sections 1 through 7 shall apply to the first election of President and Vice President held more than [two years] after the ratification of this article. Until then, the provisions repealed by Section 1 shall remain in force.
2. Sections 8 through 13 shall take effect upon ratification. After ratification, no State shall adopt any districting plan except in accordance with this article. Each State shall constitute its commission within [six] months after ratification. Every districting plan used in any election held more than [eighteen] months after ratification shall comply with this article, regardless of when it was adopted. Where a new plan is required for this purpose, it shall remain in effect until districts are drawn following the next decennial census.

## Teil C – Missbrauchsprüfung (Red Team) und Korrekturen

Methode: Ich habe zuerst eine schlichte Grundfassung geschrieben (Electoral College weg, Direktwahl, Verbot parteipolitischer Bezirkszuschnitte, Kommission). Dann habe ich sie aus der Sicht einer Partei, eines Bundesstaats, eines Kongresses und eines amtierenden Präsidenten angegriffen, die jeweils betrügen wollen. Die Spalte „Fix“ nennt die Stelle, an der das Schlupfloch in Teil B geschlossen ist.

### C.3 Restrisiken, die ein Verfassungstext nicht vollständig schließen kann

1. **Schwächen von Instant Runoff.** IRV kann nicht-monoton sein (mehr Stimmen können schaden), und Kompromisskandidaten der Mitte fliegen früh raus (Center Squeeze). Die *Option* wäre eine Condorcet-Methode wie Ranked Pairs. Die ist robuster, aber schwerer zu erklären und landesweit aufwendiger auszuzählen.
2. **Die Gerichte sind die letzte Instanz.** Fast alle Sicherungen enden vor Gericht, gegen eine politisch besetzte Justiz hilft dieser Text nur begrenzt. Die Mechanik ist deshalb bewusst so angelegt, dass Gerichte kaum Ermessen haben: Messwert, Schwelle, automatische Planauswahl.
3. **Manipulation des Zensus** (Untererfassung, Staatsbürgerschaftsfrage) wäre ein eigener Zusatzartikel.
4. **Vorwahlen und Nominierungen** der Parteien bleiben ungeregelt.
5. **Messwert und Kalibrierung.** Partisan Bias über einen gleichmäßigen Swing ist etabliert, aber nicht perfekt. Die Schwelle [5 %] ist eine politische Entscheidung, deshalb darf der Kongress nachschärfen (§9.4).
6. **Fallback-Kriterium.** Der Plan mit dem geringsten Bias gewinnt. Ein Kommissionsmitglied könnte einen Plan mit Bias nahe null einreichen, der weiche Kriterien wie Kompaktheit und Gemeindegrenzen nur knapp erfüllt. Die Gerichtsprüfung nach §10.6 fängt nur klare Verstöße ab.
7. **Bundesdurchführung bei Boykott** (§6.1) legt die Wahl in diesem Staat in die Hände einer Bundesregierung, deren Chef selbst kandidieren kann. Das ist die schlechteste, aber immer noch bessere Option als ein Staat ohne Wahl.
8. **Landesweite Nachzählung** (§6.3) bei sehr knappem Ausgang ist eine enorme logistische Aufgabe und muss innerhalb der Fristen bis zum 6. Januar zu schaffen sein.
9. **Politische Realität.** Eine Ratifizierung braucht zwei Drittel in beiden Kammern und 38 Staaten. Kleine Staaten verlieren relativ an Einfluss, die Chancen sind daher gering. Ohne Verfassungsänderung kommt man nur über den National Popular Vote Interstate Compact voran, der allerdings keines der Gerrymandering-Probleme löst.
